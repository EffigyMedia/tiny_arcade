### RLG-057 — Soviet Blocks ships modern Tetris Guideline features - an owner decision

- Description: Soviet Blocks implements a ghost piece, hold with a one-per-piece rule, a 7-bag
  randomizer and wall kicks. Its name, palette, music and block styling are original and genuinely
  distinct - the audit was explicit about that.
- **The concern is not the code, it is which era the features belong to.** These are not
  1984-abstract-rules features; they are modern Tetris Guideline behaviour, and that is trade dress
  rather than mechanics. The design document already names this machine the highest-risk clone on
  the floor, and the rights holder it descends from is the one that litigates on look and feel.
- **This is the owner's call and it is not a defect**, which is why it is a `concept` and why no
  code changed during the audit remediation pass.
- The two ways out, both defensible:
  - **Accept it** under the standing posture in [[RLG-023]] - sell the Originals, keep the clones
    free - and record that the decision was made knowingly.
  - **Drop ghost, hold and the 7-bag** for period-authentic randomness. That is also a *design*
    statement rather than a retreat: a cabinet styled as Soviet hardware arguably should not have
    the conveniences the genre grew twenty years later.
- Done when: the owner rules, and the design document's Decision Log records which and why.
