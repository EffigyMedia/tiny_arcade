### RLG-039 — The Second Wave queue - the 1990s floor
- Description: A queue of twenty-four machines from the decade after the golden age: fighters,
  shmups, run-and-guns, beat-em-ups, light-gun cabinets, racers, puzzlers, and one rhythm game.
  Names and accents are chosen; **nothing is scheduled.**
- Intent: One item so the shelf can be counted and reasoned about. Each machine becomes its own
  fragment when it is picked.
- **Read this before any of them is scheduled.** A 1980s machine is one mechanic, which is why it
  drops into a phone in a sitting. A 1990s cabinet is usually several systems at once plus a
  mountain of animation frames, and most of them assumed two hands, a stick, and six buttons. The
  work is not the systems. It is the decision about what the phone version keeps and what it
  honestly cannot.
- The recommended starting batch, recorded before the standup: **Pedal, Curtain, and Gem Duel** - a
  light-gun cabinet, a bullet-hell, and a competitive puzzler. Three genres, all natively portrait,
  all natively touch, and not one of them needs a fighting game's animation budget. The fighters and
  the beat-em-ups are where this decade gets expensive; attempt them once there is an animation
  pipeline worth the name.
- One entry supersedes another inside the queue: Downbeat replaces Sixteenth. Keep one, not both.
- Done when: the shelf has a scheduled batch, or the queue is explicitly declined.
